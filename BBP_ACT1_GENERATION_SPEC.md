# ACT 1 HEADLINE GENERATION RULES
## Beyond Bullet Points Canvas (Structured AI System)

This system generates 3 single-sentence options for each ACT 1 section of a Beyond Bullet Points narrative:

1. Setting
2. Role
3. Challenge
4. Desired end state
5. How do we get there?

The goal is to produce concise, high-impact, presentation-ready narrative statements that form a coherent story when read in sequence.

---

# PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Generate ACT 1 as a **progressive narrative argument**, not isolated sentences.

Each section must:
- Introduce new information
- Increase narrative clarity or urgency
- Move the story forward
- Work individually and as part of a sequence

---

# GLOBAL OUTPUT RULES

## Format

For each section:
- Generate 3 options
- Each option must be:
  - A single sentence
  - 80 characters or less for generation
  - One idea only
  - Standalone readable

The application validates saved Act I cards against a 90-character limit. The generation prompt uses an 80-character target to leave room for model variation.

---

## Voice & Style

Use:
- Active voice
- Present tense
- Clear, conversational language
- Audience-focused framing
- Dense, compressed phrasing

Avoid:
- Corporate jargon
- Buzzwords
- Marketing language
- Sales language
- Overly formal phrasing
- Multi-idea sentences

---

## PROHIBITED CONTENT

Do NOT include:
- Product names
- Product features
- Implementation details
- Technical architecture
- Marketing claims
- Company-centric framing
- Solution details (except How do we get there?)

---

# AUDIENCE PERSPECTIVE RULE

Determine from Project Overview.

## Direct Audience Mode (Default)
Use:
- you
- your

Example:
- You manage increasing operational complexity

## Shared Perspective Mode
Use:
- we
- our
- us

Example:
- We manage increasing operational complexity

---

## CONSISTENCY RULE

Once selected:
- Use same perspective across all five sections
- Do not mix "you" and "we"

---

# SENTENCE OPENING VARIETY RULE

Avoid repetitive sentence starts.

Do NOT default to "You..." for every sentence.

Use a mix of:
- Pronoun-led openings
- Environment-led openings
- Situation-led openings
- Pressure-led openings
- Outcome-led openings

### Examples

- Expectations continue to rise across your industry
- The pace of change keeps accelerating
- Pressure on teams continues to grow
- Critical information remains difficult to access
- A new operating approach becomes necessary

---

## VALIDATION

Across all options:
- No more than 2 sentences should start with the same word
- Sentence structure must vary
- Read naturally when spoken aloud

---

# NARRATIVE FLOW RULE (CRITICAL)

ACT 1 is a progressive argument:

Setting -> Role -> Challenge -> Desired end state -> How do we get there?

Each section must:
- Add new information
- Avoid repetition of previous sections
- Increase narrative clarity or urgency

---

## SECTION PROGRESSION LOGIC

- Setting → establishes environment
- Role → defines responsibility
- Challenge -> identifies friction or challenge
- Desired end state -> defines desired future state
- How do we get there? -> defines required shift to reach it

---

## NON-REPETITION RULE

Do NOT:
- Restate previous section in different words
- Mirror Challenge in Desired end state form
- Turn Desired end state into How do we get there? wording

Each section must ADVANCE the narrative.

---

# STAKES EVOLUTION RULE

Stakes evolve across the narrative:

- Challenge -> introduces selective consequences
- Desired end state -> shows benefit of resolving consequences
- How do we get there? -> defines shift required to unlock outcome

Do NOT reuse identical language across sections.

---

# CONCEPT COMPRESSION RULES

## Core Principle

Maximize meaning per word.

---

## COMPRESSION RULE

Each sentence must:
- Express one idea only
- Remove filler words
- Use strong verbs
- Be concrete, not abstract

---

## REMOVE FILLER

Avoid:
- in order to
- it is important
- increasingly
- very / highly / significantly (unless meaningful)
- vague intensifiers

---

## STRONG VERB RULE

Prefer:
- manage, lead, own, drive

Avoid:
- is responsible for managing → becomes "you manage"
- is facing increasing pressure → becomes "pressure is rising"

---

## ABSTRACT → CONCRETE RULE

Abstract:
- complexity is increasing

Better:
- teams manage more systems and decisions

---

## REDUNDANCY RULE

Avoid duplicate meaning:
- "faster and more efficient" → choose one if possible
- "growing and increasing" → redundant

---

## SINGLE-IDEA LOCK

Each sentence must pass:
> Can this be summarized in 5 words or fewer?

If yes → simplify further.

---

# SECTION RULES

---

# 1. SETTING

## Purpose
Define the environment the audience operates in.

## Formula
Environment + context

## Include
- Industry conditions
- External pressures
- Market dynamics
- Operational environment

## Exclude
- Problems
- Solutions
- Outcomes

## Examples
- Expectations continue to rise across your industry
- The pace of change keeps accelerating
- Pressure to deliver results continues to grow
- The environment grows more complex each year
- Work demands faster decisions than ever

---

# 2. ROLE

## Purpose
Define audience responsibility.

## Formula
Audience + responsibility

## Include
- Accountability
- Ownership
- Leadership
- Mission

## Exclude
- Problems
- Outcomes
- Frustration

## Examples
- You manage increasing operational complexity
- Your team depends on you for consistent delivery
- You are responsible for critical decisions
- Maintaining performance across priorities falls to you
- Success depends on your ability to balance demands

---

# 3. CHALLENGE

## Purpose
Describe current friction or limitation.

## Formula
Challenge OR challenge + implied consequence

## Include
- Friction
- Inefficiency
- Constraints
- Risks (selectively)

---

## OPTIONAL STAKES RULE

Use stakes selectively:
- At least 1 option must be standard challenge
- At least 2 options should include stakes (when contextually appropriate)
- Not all options should escalate urgency

---

## STAKES STYLE

Prefer:
- grounded consequences
- operational/business impact
- implicit rather than dramatic phrasing

Avoid:
- exaggeration
- fear-based language
- generic consequences

---

## STAKES QUALITY RULE

Stakes must:
- Be context-specific
- Not be interchangeable across scenarios
- Vary across dimensions (speed, cost, risk, growth, experience)
- Stay grounded in reality

---

## EXAMPLES

### Standard
- Critical information remains difficult to access
- Too much time is spent reacting instead of planning
- Visibility limits confident decision-making
- Growing demands strain existing processes
- Keeping pace becomes harder each year

### Stakes-Aware
- Delayed decisions reduces your competitive responsiveness
- Limited visibility slows action when timing matters
- Inefficient workflows increase your operational strain
- Missed insights weaken decision confidence over time
- Complexity makes coordination harder across teams

---

# 4. DESIRED END STATE

## Purpose
Define future state.

## Formula
Outcome state

## Include
- Improved capability
- Success state
- Operational benefit

## Exclude
- Methods
- Tools
- Implementation

## Examples
- Confident decisions become possible with full visibility
- Your teams operate with greater alignment and clarity
- Execution becomes faster without added complexity
- Strategy replaces reactive decision-making
- Growth becomes easier to manage operationally

---

# 5. HOW DO WE GET THERE?

## Purpose
Define required shift to move from Challenge to Desired end state.

## Formula
Strategic action or mindset shift

## Include
- Decisions
- Commitments
- Strategic shifts
- Organizational change

## Exclude
- Product pitches
- Benefits
- Outcomes already stated in Desired end state

## Examples
- A more proactive approach becomes essential
- It's time to rethink your current operating models
- Greater alignment is needed to move you forward
- A shift toward scalable strategy is required
- You must adopt a more adaptive approach

---

# CROSS-SECTION ESCALATION LOGIC

ACT 1 is a progressive argument:

Setting -> Role -> Challenge -> Desired end state -> How do we get there?

Each step must:
- Increase clarity
- Increase specificity
- Increase narrative weight
- Introduce new information

---

## PROGRESSION RULE

Each section must NOT:
- Repeat previous ideas
- Restate the same concept in different words
- Mirror structure across sections

---

## FAILURE MODES

Avoid:
- “same idea, different wording”
- How do we get there? repeating Desired end state
- Desired end state mirroring Challenge positively
- over-symmetry across sections

---

# FINAL VALIDATION CHECK

Before output:

- 3 options per section
- 80 characters or less per generated sentence
- 90 characters or less per saved Act I card
- One idea per sentence
- Consistent perspective
- No repetition across sections
- Narrative flows naturally from Setting to How do we get there?
- Sentences are compressed, not verbose
- Each line sounds natural when spoken aloud
